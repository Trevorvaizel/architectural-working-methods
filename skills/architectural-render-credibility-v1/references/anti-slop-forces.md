# Anti-Slop Forces

Use these forces to diagnose a visually fluent render that still feels false.

## 1. Statistical Gravity

Broad words such as `premium`, `cinematic`, `luxury`, `Japandi`, and `editorial` pull generation toward internet-average beige hospitality imagery.

**Test:** Could the image belong to another project after changing the logo?

**Correction:** Replace vibe words with specific client, climate, typology, material, ritual, and emotional decisions.

## 2. Causal Debt

Appearance is not automatically evidence of a physical cause.

**Tells:** unmotivated glow, bright frame edges, unsupported elements, impossible reflection, uniform material response.

**Correction:** Require source, receiver, geometry, material response, and scene-wide consequence for each visual event.

## 3. Fidelity Drift

Realism improves while the approved design mutates.

**Tells:** changed opening count, altered ceiling planes, different mullion rhythm, resized door, warped tile, recentered camera.

**Correction:** Pin the first trustworthy design render or drawing as `O0`. For the second AI revision and later, return to `O0` and reapply approved deltas; use generated work only as a named lighting, material, styling, or mood reference. Never repair drift by generating from the drifted derivative again.

For same-house render stacks, separate two truths: each view has its own `O0-viewNN` for geometry, while the first approved render `P01` becomes project canon for shared visual decisions. Later views must not inherit `P01` geometry, but they must inherit its approved material passports, lighting language, fixture family, styling restraint, and photographic grade.

## 4. Coherence Horizon

A single frame can conceal incomplete spatial understanding.

**Tells:** materials, windows, fixtures, scale, or context cannot survive another view or revision.

**Correction:** Treat the coordinated view set and revision chain as the unit of truth. Maintain a canon ledger from approved views `P01`, `P02`, and onward so render `10` still belongs to the same house without copying the camera or composition of render `01`.

## 5. Motivated Light

Light must be caused, not painted decoratively.

**Tells:** pools without fixtures, hard shadows behind sheers, practicals overpowering daylight, reflections with no matching source.

**Correction:** Keep a source ledger and establish a dominant-source hierarchy.

## 6. Material Identity

A material must remain itself under different light and view conditions.

**Tells:** timber, plaster, fabric, and metal share one sheen; grain changes direction; texture scale drifts; curtains glow like paper.

**Correction:** Use material passports and distinct roughness, edge, thickness, and joint behavior.

For exterior renders, vegetation is also a material system. It must show plant bodies, stems, soil or mulch gaps, scale changes, contact shadows, and distance-based detail falloff. A continuous fuzzy green surface, repeated leaf stamp, or equal sharpness from foreground to background is material failure, not lushness.

## 7. Construction Residue

Real minimalism contains disciplined joints and systems; it is not missing information.

**Tells:** planes meet without reveals, fixtures lack attachment, glass lacks thickness, curtains lack track/hem, doors lack frame or hardware, service elements disappear.

**Correction:** Inspect every material transition for fixing, separation, opening, sealing, powering, cleaning, and replacement logic.

## 8. Correlated Imperfection

Real variation follows use, fabrication, gravity, moisture, handling, age, and cleaning.

**Tells:** sterile perfection or uniformly scattered knots, scratches, dirt, props, and noise.

**Correction:** Place restrained variation only where a real cause predicts it.

## 9. Typological Pressure

References carry social scripts with their style.

**Tells:** a private residence becomes a hotel through repeated sconces, full curtain walls, symmetrical art, spa lighting, or showroom staging.

**Correction:** Write the typology and ritual sentence before styling.

## 10. Truth Gradient

Photorealism can make uncertainty look resolved.

**Tells:** mood studies are presented as final approval evidence; invented filler is not distinguished from designed content.

**Correction:** Declare concept, material study, design development, or approval status.

## Forbidden Signatures

- geometry changed to add realism;
- bent or competing vanishing lines;
- warped repeating patterns;
- unidentified luminous strips or thresholds;
- every fixture active in strong daylight;
- uniform amber color wash;
- a single gloss response across the room;
- repeated abstract art and generic styling filler;
- continuous gauzy curtain walls without project logic;
- mirror floors that erase their own pattern;
- artificial dirt used as proof of realism;
- hyper-sharp detail everywhere;
- vegetation fused into carpet, paste, or texture noise;
- approved cars, people, signage, planters, or balcony pots disappearing between revisions;
- finality claimed without source-backed continuity.
